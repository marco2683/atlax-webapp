# AtlasDT Website Clarity & Value Proposition Rebuild — Antigravity Implementation Prompt

## Paste this prompt into Antigravity

You are working on the AtlasDT website codebase. Your task is to thoroughly update the entire website so that it becomes clearer, more credible, more commercially focused, and better targeted to specific customer niches.

The website must no longer feel like a broad unfinished ecosystem trying to be a design agency, sourcing broker, SaaS platform, marketplace, quote engine, talent marketplace, and OEM catalogue all at once. The new website must present AtlasDT as a focused, engineering-led product realisation and Asian manufacturing partner.

The core concept to optimise for is:

> **Clarity of proposition and value.**

A potential customer should understand within 5 seconds:

1. What AtlasDT does.
2. Who AtlasDT helps.
3. What problem AtlasDT solves.
4. Why AtlasDT is credible.
5. What action they should take next.

# 1. Strategic Positioning

## 1.1 Primary positioning statement

Use this as the north star for the site:

> **AtlasDT helps hardware companies design, prototype, source, cost-down, and manufacture physical products in Asia — with engineering control from Melbourne and supplier execution in China.**

Alternative shorter headline:

> **Design, source, and manufacture hardware in Asia — without losing technical control.**

Alternative commercial headline:

> **From product idea to Asian production, with engineering-led DFM, sourcing, and manufacturing execution.**

## 1.2 What AtlasDT should be perceived as

AtlasDT should be perceived as:

- an engineering-led product realisation partner;
- a bridge between Western product teams and Asian manufacturing capability;
- a technical sourcing and manufacturing execution partner;
- a DFM, cost-down, supplier qualification, and production support company;
- a Melbourne-led company with on-the-ground China / Pearl River Delta supplier access.

AtlasDT should **not** primarily be perceived as:

- a generic marketplace;
- a supplier directory;
- an Alibaba-style catalogue;
- an unfinished SaaS platform;
- a trading company;
- a simple broker;
- a design studio only;
- a generic consultancy.

## 1.3 One-sentence description

Use this consistently across metadata, homepage, footer, and about page:

> AtlasDT helps founders, product teams, and industrial companies turn hardware ideas, CAD files, and prototypes into manufacturable, cost-effective products through engineering-led design, DFM, sourcing, and production support in Asia.

## 1.4 Value proposition hierarchy

The site must emphasise value in this order:

1. **Reduce manufacturing risk** — avoid bad suppliers, poor handovers, weak quality control, and unmanufacturable designs.
2. **Reduce cost** — use DFM, supplier comparison, Asian manufacturing access, and cost-down reviews.
3. **Move faster from prototype to production** — clarify next steps, documentation, supplier RFQs, and pilot production.
4. **Keep technical control** — engineering-led process instead of blind outsourcing.
5. **Access vetted suppliers** — China / Asia supplier network supported by technical review and on-the-ground execution.

## 7.1 Objective

## 7.3 Process steps

Use the six-step process:

1. **Discovery Call**
   - Understand product, stage, goals, target cost, timeline, risks.

2. **Technical Intake**
   - Review CAD, drawings, BOM, photos, samples, supplier quotes, or product brief.

3. **Manufacturing Strategy**
   - Recommend processes, materials, DFM improvements, cost-down opportunities, and project pathway.

4. **RFQ & Supplier Matching**
   - Build supplier-ready documentation and approach suitable factories.

5. **Prototype / Sample / Pilot**
   - Coordinate technical feedback, sample review, and production readiness.

6. **Production Support**
   - Support quality checks, production communication, packaging, logistics, and issue resolution.

## 7.4 Engagement models

Add clear engagement types.

### Product Feasibility Review

Best for early-stage ideas.

**Output:** feasibility notes, manufacturing route, development roadmap.

### DFM & Cost-Down Sprint

Best for existing CAD, prototypes, or expensive products.

**Output:** DFM report, cost-down opportunities, RFQ readiness.

### Supplier Sourcing Project

Best for products ready to quote.

**Output:** supplier shortlist, comparable quotes, recommendation.

### Production Support Retainer

Best for ongoing supplier management, sampling, QC, or production follow-up.

**Output:** ongoing technical and supplier execution support.

Do not add exact pricing unless the business is ready. You may use “quoted after discovery” or “fixed-scope options available.”

# 16. Technical Implementation Requirements

## 16.1 Inspect existing code first

Before making changes, inspect the current project structure, routes, components, content files, and styling system.

Identify:

- framework being used;
- routing structure;
- reusable components;
- CMS/content structure if any;
- existing pages;
- current app/platform modules;
- where navigation/footer are defined;
- where SEO metadata is set.

## 16.2 Preserve working functionality

Do not break working pages or existing app architecture. If modules are unfinished, hide them from public navigation rather than deleting them unless deletion is clearly safe.

## 16.3 Clean up public-facing issues

Fix:

- spelling mistakes;
- inconsistent capitalization;
- “Get Intouch” to “Get in Touch”;
- inconsistent supplier numbers;
- placeholder addresses;
- demo content;
- placeholder cards;
- empty marketplace states;
- zero-value checkout previews;
- repeated FAQ sections;
- loading-only blog pages.

## 16.4 Add feature gating for unfinished platform areas

If platform modules are not ready, implement one of:

- `isPrivateBeta` flag;
- login required;
- coming-soon marketing wrapper;
- hidden route from public navigation;
- demo screenshots only.

## 16.5 Components to create or reuse

Create reusable components if appropriate:

- `HeroSection`
- `CustomerPathwayCards`
- `ServiceCard`
- `DeliverablesList`
- `ProcessSteps`
- `TrustProofSection`
- `CaseStudyMiniCard`
- `CTASection`
- `FAQAccordion`
- `ProjectIntakeForm`
- `PlatformModuleCard`
- `ComparisonTable`

Keep the code maintainable and avoid hardcoding duplicated copy in many places if the project has a content/config system.

# 19. Conversion Strategy

## 19.1 Primary conversion paths

Create four major conversion paths:

1. **Idea / early concept** → Product Feasibility Review
2. **CAD / prototype** → DFM & Prototype Review
3. **Ready to manufacture** → Supplier Sourcing Project
4. **Existing product too expensive** → Cost-Down Review

## 19.2 CTA mapping

| Customer intent | CTA | Destination |
|---|---|---|
| I have an idea | Start Idea Review | Contact form with stage preselected |
| I have CAD | Upload CAD / BOM | Contact form with upload field |
| I need suppliers | Request Supplier Sourcing | Contact form with supplier sourcing selected |
| Product costs too much | Request Cost-Down Review | Contact form with cost-down selected |
| I have supplier issues | Fix Manufacturing Problem | Contact form with issue category selected |

## 19.3 Form behaviour

If possible, prefill project stage or inquiry type based on CTA clicked.

Example:

- `/contact?stage=idea`
- `/contact?stage=dfm`
- `/contact?stage=sourcing`
- `/contact?stage=cost-down`
- `/contact?stage=supplier-issue`

# 20. Acceptance Criteria

The website update is complete only when the following are true:

## 20.1 Clarity

- A visitor can understand what AtlasDT does within 5 seconds.
- The homepage has one clear value proposition.
- The four customer pathways are visible and easy to understand.
- The services are reduced to four clear primary categories.
- The platform is secondary to the service proposition.

## 20.2 Credibility

- No public placeholder text remains.
- No empty marketplace or 0-result product sections are publicly visible.
- No fake/demo content is visible publicly.
- Supplier numbers are consistent and honestly framed.
- Location information is credible and not placeholder-like.
- The site includes proof blocks, sample deliverables, or anonymised examples.

## 20.3 Conversion

- Every major page has one primary CTA.
- Contact page includes a project intake form.
- CTA labels are specific, not generic.
- Customer-stage CTAs route to the correct inquiry type where possible.

## 20.4 Content quality

- FAQ is concise and buyer-focused.
- Blog/resources page is either populated with useful content or hidden until ready.
- Copy is direct, practical, and technical enough to be credible.
- Abstract “ecosystem/platform” language is reduced.
- Deliverables are listed for each service.

## 20.5 Technical quality

- Navigation and footer are updated consistently.
- SEO metadata is updated.
- Pages are responsive.
- Existing working functionality is preserved.
- Unfinished app/platform modules are gated or hidden.
- No broken links or loading-only public pages remain.

# 21. Final Instruction to Antigravity

Implement the above changes thoroughly across the whole website. Prioritise customer clarity, practical value, credibility, and conversion over feature breadth.

The final website should make AtlasDT feel like a serious engineering-led partner for companies that need help designing, sourcing, cost-reducing, and manufacturing physical products in Asia.

Do not make the site feel like a generic marketplace or unfinished SaaS dashboard. The marketplace, quote engine, supplier register, and platform modules may exist, but they must support the central proposition rather than compete with it.

When uncertain, choose the option that makes the site clearer, more credible, and easier for a real customer to act on.
